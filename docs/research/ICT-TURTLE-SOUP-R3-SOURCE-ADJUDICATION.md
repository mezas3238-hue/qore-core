Post-holdout forensics showed broad failure plus frequent post-stop recovery. DeepSeek then proposed a stricter Turtle Soup definition. ## TTrades corroboration

TTrades' reversal-sequence material places `Purge / Turtle Soup` first as the liquidity-sweep/reversal event. It then presents inversion, CISD, FVG and breaker as later stages / alternative confirmation families. TTrades explicitly distinguishes confirmation preference:

- lower confirmation: entry after liquidity sweep / Turtle Soup;
- medium confirmation: inversion or CISD;
- higher confirmation: FVG or breaker.

Therefore `CISD/MSS/BOS/FVG mandatory to define Turtle Soup` is not supported by TTrades itself.

TTrades CISD material does strongly support the opposing-candle-series concept: bullish CISD closes above the opening price of the relevant down-close series; bearish CISD closes below the opening price of the relevant up-close series. When multiple candles form the series, TTrades points to the opening price of the first candle in that sequence.

TTrades also supports top-down context, higher-timeframe levels, protected swings and directional alignment for specific execution models. Those are useful candidate context/confirmation components, but they must not be misrepresented as universal definitional requirements for every Turtle Soup.

## Adjudication of the DeepSeek report

### Accepted as a strong R3 research direction

1. **R2 likely over-generalized liquidity references.** Completed session highs/lows, previous-day/week levels and other pools were treated too uniformly. A source-bound R3 should distinguish meaningful old/pre-existing liquidity from merely available extrema.
2. **Context matters.** ICT and TTrades both support contextual narrative / higher-timeframe framing. The exact deterministic bias algorithm remains a QORE formalization problem.
3. **The quality and persistence of rejection matters.** R2 forensics showed many entries move favorably and later fail. R3 should characterize rejection/persistence explicitly rather than assume a sweep plus CISD is sufficient.
4. **Multiple pools should not automatically be called invalid.** Source evidence does not support R2's blanket abstention simply because more than one meaningful liquidity reference is clustered at the swept price. Confluence may be meaningful, but deterministic ranking remains unresolved.

### Rejected as universal source claims

- `London/NY-AM Kill Zone required` — contradicted by primary ICT Lunch Macro and NY PM Turtle Soup examples.
- `D1/H4 ERL only` — over-specified; primary ICT material includes old short-term liquidity and equal highs in Turtle Soup discussion.
- `Short-term high/low cannot be Turtle Soup` — contradicted by primary ICT discussion of an old short-term high in the Turtle Soup sequence.
- `CISD/MSS/BOS mandatory to define Turtle Soup` — not supported; TTrades explicitly treats these as later confirmation choices.
- `Displacement/FVG mandatory to define Turtle Soup` — not supported as a universal definition. They may be candidate confirmation-quality features.
- `1–2 pips/ticks penetration` — source-underdetermined and not transferable across FX, indices and metals.
- `pool age >= 3 bars` — unsupported quantization.
- `equal highs/lows tolerance <= 5 points` — unsupported universal quantization.
- `same-candle reclaim mandatory` — not established by primary ICT evidence recovered.
- `wick > 50%` — unsupported quantization.
- `minimum 2R` — execution-model choice, not Turtle Soup definition.
- `entry exactly at CISD close` — candidate execution variant, not universal source rule.

## Current source-bound Turtle Soup core

The strongest common denominator is:

`pre-existing meaningful high/low liquidity -> run/violation -> failure/rejection -> repricing away toward opposing liquidity`

Optional source-compatible confirmation layers can include:

- CISD / protected swing;
- MSS;
- inversion;
- FVG / breaker;
- higher-timeframe narrative / point of interest;
- session/macro context.

These must be tested as preregistered candidate families rather than silently bundled into the definition.

## What R2 most likely got wrong

R2 did not necessarily fail because Turtle Soup needs a single mandatory Kill Zone or a D1/H4-only pool. The more defensible source-derived diagnosis is:

1. it treated several time/reference extrema as economically equivalent liquidity triggers;
2. it lacked a source-bound concept of `meaningful/old/obvious` liquidity quality;
3. it used one confirmation family (CISD) as if it were sufficient to establish persistence;
4. it used a single mechanical invalidation despite evidence that many paths retest deeply after an initial favorable response;
5. it rejected multi-pool sweeps as ambiguous even though stacked liquidity may be contextually meaningful;
6. it had no explicit separation between Turtle Soup as the liquidity event and the later entry-confirmation model.

## R3 source hypothesis

R3 will test the hypothesis that economic quality depends less on a universal clock gate and more on whether the sweep raids a genuinely meaningful pre-existing liquidity reference and then demonstrates a source-compatible rejection/confirmation sequence.

Candidate features may be researched on consumed evidence descriptively, but no feature becomes an economic rule until it is frozen under a new candidate identity and tested on different untouched evidence.
